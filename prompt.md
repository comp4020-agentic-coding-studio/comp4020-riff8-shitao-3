# Trace: The Museum of Things We Keep

Turn Trace at https://comp4020-riff8-shitao-3.fly.dev/ into an anonymous, living museum of ordinary objects people choose to keep. Combine the Anonymous Museum and Drawing Fingerprints ideas into one experience: every exhibit contains a visitor's drawing of an object, a short account of why it matters, and a visual "trace seal" generated from how the sketch was drawn. The sketch tells us what someone kept, the story tells us why, and the seal lets us explore the movement that made it. Make the museum itself the main experience, with contributions appearing for everyone in real time and remaining available when people return. Build the complete collection, transformation, storage and browsing flow. Make reasonable decisions and finish without needing follow-up answers.

The governing requirement is to gather information A and show it to users in form B. Here, information A is deliberately supplied object information plus measured drawing gestures: an object label, how long the visitor has kept it, how it came to them, why they keep it, a chosen reason tag, and the sketch's coordinates and relative timing. Form B is a shared illustrated museum, arranged into cabinets of time kept, where every object has a generated seal and can be connected to other objects through either its story or its drawing rhythm. Every visual encoding must have an understandable explanation. A visitor should be able to point to a feature of an exhibit and say which collected information produced it.

Start by inspecting README.md, CLAUDE.md, package.json, fly.toml, the current source, public assets, spec/ and CI. The current app uses a server-issued anonymous hand cookie in src/identity.ts, SQLite in src/db.ts, page rendering in src/pages.ts, routes in src/server.ts, and public/wall.js for drawing and receiving committed marks over /api/marks/stream. Extend the existing Node, TypeScript and locally served JavaScript structure. Preserve the pod-riff instructions at the top of CLAUDE.md and the harness-owned agent/ files. This brief authorizes bounded plain-text exhibit labels and stories, intentional drawing measurements, and multi-stroke museum sketches. Rewrite the older README argument and corresponding harness rules that prohibit all text before implementing those changes, and update affected tests to express the new promises honestly. Keep anonymous cookie identity, same-origin assets, server-side limits, persistent storage and persistence before broadcasts.

Keep the original shared wall available at /wall/, preserving every existing mark, its pen and colour, keyboard drawing, private recognition of a hand's own marks, and its current rolling one-mark-per-24-hours rule. Make the museum the home page and link clearly between the museum, the original wall and /readme/. Museum submissions have their own independent rolling limit of one published exhibit per hand per 24 hours; the original wall allowance is unchanged. A museum exhibit can contain several strokes, all prepared privately and published together. Enforce both limits on the server, make museum submission and its limit check atomic, and do not let deleting an exhibit reset its publication cooldown. Drafting, undoing and trying again never consume an allowance.

Use the title "The Museum of Things We Keep" and a short invitation such as "Draw something you kept. Leave the story of why." Give the museum the character of a carefully arranged cabinet of curiosities: warm paper, clear frames, generous space and restrained ink colours that carry through from Trace. Make the user's drawing the centre of each exhibit, with its trace seal integrated into the frame or displayed alongside it at a consistent readable size. The museum should feel inviting with two objects and remain usable with hundreds. On phones, cabinets can become a vertical sequence of shelves with the same meaning. Support both colour schemes with clear contrast. Do not require a sprawling three-dimensional scene or constant animation to make it interesting.

The contribution flow should be easy to complete: choose an object you own or remember keeping, draw it, add its label and short story, preview the complete exhibit, then explicitly publish it. Collect a plain-text label of at most 60 characters and a story of at most 240 characters answering "Why did you keep it?" Require meaningful nonempty values, without enforcing fluent English or a minimum artistic standard. Ask how long the visitor has kept the object, using "Under 1 year", "1 to under 5 years", "5 to under 10 years", "10 years or more" and "Not sure". This describes time kept, not the object's manufacture date. Collect its origin from gift, bought, found, made, inherited, other or "Not sure", and one reason from memory, comfort, usefulness, connection or curiosity. Store uncertain origin as a distinct unknown enum value and display an "Origin uncertain" badge. A drawing, label and story are required; uncertainty is a valid response to duration and origin. Show before publishing that the exhibit, story and drawing-motion visualization will be public. Encourage stories about the object without personal contact details, and never ask for a real name, photograph or location.

Provide a dedicated museum drawing surface with multiple strokes, the existing pen and colour choices, undo last stroke, clear, and a preview. Mouse, touch, stylus and the existing keyboard drawing pattern must all work. Transform coordinates into a fixed drawing space using the actual SVG or canvas transform, including letterboxing, page scroll and resize. Avoid disturbing the original wall client when sharing drawing utilities. A cancelled gesture should leave no accidental stroke. Undo must remove that stroke's motion contribution as well as its geometry, and clearing must reset both. Keep drafts private, recover an unfinished draft in the same browser after an accidental reload, and provide a clear way to discard it. Do not broadcast drafts or quietly submit a drawing on pointer release. The final publish action submits the entire previewed exhibit once; failure leaves the draft available for retry.

A drawing fingerprint means a visual description of this particular submitted sketch. Measure drawing movement only inside the deliberate museum drawing flow. Record bounded stroke points in normalized drawing coordinates with elapsed timestamps from a monotonic clock such as performance.now(), capturing stroke starts and ends as well as movement. Measure total travelled distance, active stroke duration, movement speed, meaningful pauses within a held stroke, and changes of direction. Define the units, sampling and pause threshold in the code and README. For example, a pause is at least 500 milliseconds of negligible movement while a stroke remains active; time between separate strokes is excluded. Capture stationary intervals deliberately rather than assuming pointermove events will arrive while the pointer rests. Stop or cancel an active gesture when the page hides or input is cancelled so hidden-tab time and a suspended browser do not become a giant pause. Preserve genuine pauses while making calculations robust to zero distance, repeated points, duplicate timestamps, jitter and irregular event rates. Use consistent resampling and thresholds in normalized coordinates so the result is not simply the frequency of device events. Consult current [Pointer Events](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Pointer_events) and [performance.now()](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Performance/now) documentation for the implementation.

Generate a compact circular trace seal with a fixed interpretation. Reading clockwise follows the recorded sketch progress; radial variation represents movement speed, visible hollow beads mark qualifying pauses, and an inner band represents the amount of turning in the path. Use a fixed documented scale across exhibits, with out-of-range values visibly capped, so two seals can be compared. Keep stroke boundaries identifiable. The drawing's selected ink supplies its appearance, while the seal's shape carries the measurements. Preview the seal before publication, and compute or verify the final descriptor from validated submitted samples on the server, rather than accepting arbitrary finished statistics from the client. Store the descriptor version so older exhibits remain interpretable when calculations change. On the exhibit page show a short plain-language explanation and numeric values with units. Input method affects motion: record mouse, touch, pen, keyboard or mixed input as exhibit metadata and show it on inspection. Never treat a seal as an identifier, a personality diagnosis, a skill score or evidence about the strength of someone's feelings.

Organize the main museum into the five duration cabinets, including a clearly labelled "Time uncertain" cabinet. Within each, display framed sketches with their labels and trace seals. A small origin badge explains whether an object was gifted, bought, found, made, inherited, otherwise acquired or of uncertain origin; reason tags have readable text as well as colour. Opening an exhibit reveals its full drawing, story, time-kept category, origin, reason and fingerprint explanation together. Use public exhibit accession numbers such as "Object 0042", with no author name or stable public visitor profile. Highlight "your exhibit" privately in its owner's browser and provide an owner-authorized withdrawal action. Store any association with the hand privately, never place cookie IDs or hashes of them in public pages, endpoints, event streams or similarity links, and do not publicly group a visitor's exhibits together.

The distinctive exploration feature is "Follow a thread" on each exhibit. Offer "Same reason, another object" to open another exhibit with the same explicitly chosen reason tag, and "Similar gesture, another story" to compare a different exhibit with a similar measured movement descriptor. The second connection should use a small, documented deterministic distance over movement-speed, pause and turning features, with fixed normalization and a stable tie-break. Compare only compatible input methods and explicitly exclude the current exhibit; a same-owner exclusion may be applied privately without revealing the association. Show the actual reason for the connection, such as "Both were kept for connection" or "Both sketches have similar pause proportions and turning", with their measurements side by side. A connection describes submitted information, not an inferred relationship between people. Make absent matches a friendly, honest state rather than manufacturing them. With only one exhibit the user can still inspect it fully; with two suitable exhibits the thread can work immediately. Do not use an external AI service to classify stories or infer objects.

Build one particularly memorable discovery into that comparison: let a visitor switch between "The objects" and "The gestures" without losing the pair. The first view places two sketches and their stories side by side; the second aligns their trace seals on the same scales and explains their shared or different features. This makes it possible to discover that unrelated objects were drawn with similar rhythms, or that objects kept for the same reason were drawn very differently. Use a gentle, optional reveal animation, with a static equivalent for reduced motion. For example, someone draws a chipped mug inherited from a grandparent and selects connection. Another visitor draws a concert ticket given by a friend and also selects connection. Their exhibits share a story thread, while their seals may show different movement patterns. Treat this as an illustrative walkthrough only: do not create fictional exhibits or invented movement data in the live collection. Put controlled fixtures only in tests or a clearly separate development environment.

When someone publishes an exhibit, the complete sketch, story and seal must become available to other open museum sessions within about one second, without a reload. Save the entire validated exhibit before emitting its public event, and reconcile HTTP completion with stream delivery using stable exhibit IDs and an idempotent publication token so retries and echoes produce one exhibit. Reconnect must recover missed additions and withdrawals, and initial snapshot/stream races must not lose contributions. Choose distinct museum events or a dedicated same-origin stream so existing wall mark consumers continue to understand their own events. Keep a viewer's open exhibit and scroll position stable when another contribution arrives; update relevant cabinet counts and insert the new exhibit without hijacking the current interaction. Withdrawals must remove public geometry, story, descriptor and thread eligibility from storage and open views. Deleting one exhibit must not delete unrelated marks or exhibits. Two simultaneous submissions from the same hand must produce at most one new exhibit, while different hands can publish concurrently.

Implement public text as escaped plain text in every page, attribute, API consumer and event-driven renderer. Validate enums, lengths, finite in-bounds coordinates, pen and colour choices, monotonic timestamps, stroke counts, sample counts and plausible timing limits; bound request bodies and work done by each request. Use parameterized SQLite queries, cookie-derived ownership, same-origin write protection, rate limits and transaction-based publication rules. A client can falsify its own drawing timing, so label these as recorded sketch estimates and never use them for entitlements or artist rankings. Add a simple persistent report/hide mechanism with bounded reasons, server-side deduplication per hand, and a documented review path for reported public stories. One report must not automatically erase another person's exhibit, and readers must be able to hide a reported item locally while it awaits review. Do not add public comments, reactions, followers, leaderboards, accounts, uploads, third-party tracking or paid services. The only captured movement is what a visitor chooses to contribute as an exhibit.

Persist exhibits and their required bounded drawing geometry, relative timing representation, descriptors, metadata and publication timestamps in the existing DB_PATH SQLite database on the Fly volume. Keep existing hands and marks through an idempotent migration and make the archive of original drawings available immediately. Historical wall marks have no recorded timing or object stories; leave them in the original wall without inventing fingerprints or museum metadata. Published museum exhibits persist across sessions, restarts and redeploys until withdrawn. Keep withdrawal cooldown records separately from publicly visible content. Fit the existing single 256 MB Fly machine with pagination, indexed cabinet and tag queries, bounded comparison work and locally served SVG or canvas rendering. Use no new infrastructure. Timings need only a compact relative representation sufficient to reproduce the seal; do not retain a stream of general site activity. Make individual exhibits directly linkable by their public accession ID, ensure all cabinets are browseable, and avoid downloading the entire museum merely to open one exhibit.

Make creation, publication, cabinet browsing, exhibit inspection, comparison, reporting and withdrawal usable with a keyboard and on a phone. Provide visible focus, meaningful field labels, understandable errors, a readable text alternative to the seal and a simple legend for each visual encoding. Match an exhibit's accessible description to its supplied label and story rather than guessing what the drawing depicts. Input-method differences must not exclude people from contributing or seeing their seal; when a comparison lacks compatible data, say so clearly. Respect reduced motion, maintain contrast in both colour schemes and never make speed, colour or fine pointer control the only way to understand the museum.

Verify the collected-information-to-visual-form contract with deterministic fixtures before accepting the implementation: identical submitted samples produce identical seals; a slower timed version of the same path changes the speed encoding as documented; a held stationary interval creates a pause marker; added turns change the turning feature; undo removes geometry and metrics together; and resizing preserves the intended normalized geometry. Confirm that uncertain duration and origin publish successfully and render their correct cabinet and badge. Cover invalid and duplicate timestamps, sparse strokes, mixed input, hidden-tab interruption, malicious text, oversized payloads, cookie ownership, report deduplication, single-hand concurrent publishing, duplicate retries, snapshot/stream races, reconnect recovery, withdrawals, migration and restart persistence. Use meaningful tests to reproduce failures, then implement and verify. Run pnpm check with the configured toolchain, keep spec/invariants.test.ts green, and complete the applicable CI checks. In real browser sessions with separate cookie jars, publish two actual exhibits, see them arrive in the other session, open a thread, switch the comparison view, test keyboard drawing, withdraw an owned exhibit and return after a restart. Judge the layout with sparse and populated test collections at desktop and mobile sizes. Update README.md, the relevant harness rules and tests so the information gathered, encodings, anonymity limits and definition of a good museum agree.
